I am a professor of computer science at Reykjavik University. Most of my career has been
in the theory of algorithms, and that work has its own home at
[magnusmh.is](https://magnusmh.is). This site is for the other half: what AI is doing to
university education, and what I am doing about it at RU. I chair the university's
Curriculum Council, teach a first-year course built around active, group-based
problem-solving, and run a number of efforts to get colleagues oriented in a fast-moving
field.

Each dispatch here is a short public summary of one of those efforts, written when it
reaches a state worth reporting. The rule for what appears is simple: my own positions,
public outputs, and outlines. Nothing from behind closed doors, and colleagues only get a
nod.

## How these are written

The dispatches are drafted with Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant, working from my own
notes, scripts and records. I read every draft, correct it, and sign it off before it
appears; nothing is published that I have not been through. The positions are mine, the
first draft usually is not, and I would rather say so than have it inferred.

## Why "vélgreind"

The usual Icelandic word for artificial intelligence is *gervigreind*, where *gervi-*
means artificial in the sense of fake or imitation. I think that prefix has aged badly.
*Vélgreind*, machine intelligence, says what the thing is without passing judgment on it,
and it follows the older coinage *vitvél* (intelligent machine) that the Icelandic
Institute for Intelligent Machines, Vitvélastofnun Íslands, has used for years.
